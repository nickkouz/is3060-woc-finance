How Research Requests Move Through the Fund Today

Analysts ask pod leads for research on merger, spin-off, and other event-driven situations. This page describes the current, mostly informal process: requests come in by message, get picked up when someone sees them, and are tracked in a shared spreadsheet.

See the process

About the Process

The research request process is how an analyst gets help from the fund on a specific event-driven situation, such as a pending acquisition or a spin-off. The fund trades real capital, so research needs to be timely and consistent.

Process Users

  • Analysts who request research
  • Pod leads who assign and review it
  • Portfolio managers who use the output
  • Faculty advisor, who oversees the fund

Inputs and Resources

  • Message or email describing the situation
  • SEC filings and the deal announcement
  • Shared tracking spreadsheet
  • Analyst and pod lead time

Activities

  1. Analyst messages a pod lead
  2. Pod lead replies when available
  3. Request is added to the spreadsheet
  4. Someone researches and posts a memo

Outputs

A short research memo on the situation and a recommendation on whether to take a position.

Customers

The portfolio managers and the fund as a whole, who rely on the memo to decide how to allocate capital.

Current Information

Notice: Requests are currently sent by message. If you do not hear back within a day or two, follow up with your pod lead directly.
  • How to ask: Message your pod lead with the company name and a short description of the situation.
  • How to check status: Open the shared tracking spreadsheet and look for your row, if one was added.
  • Typical wait: Varies. Requests that arrive during exam weeks can wait several days.
  • Known issue: Two analysts sometimes research the same company without knowing it.
